#### 2026 SNAP Congressional District Visits

Building on the success of the SNAP Congressional District Visits initiative that took place during the [2025 August recess](https://snapcoalition.org/initiatives/district-visits-2025/), SNAP is [organizing a 2026 round](https://snapcoalition.org/initiatives/district-visits-2026/) of congressional district visits! This year, visits are particularly **focused on the proposed [OMB](https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/) (Office of Management and Budget) federal financial assistance rule** ([Docket OMB-2026-0034](https://www.regulations.gov/docket/OMB-2026-0034)) and its potential negative impacts on scientific researchers and the public.

#### Science Policy in a SNAP - SNAP Blog

You can access our blog, Science Policy in a SNAP, [on our website](https://snapcoalition.org/blog/) or give us [a follow on Medium](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap), where you'll hear from a variety of SNAP member voices on diverse science and science-policy topics.

Earlier this month, in their article, ""**Why build a $4B space telescope?**," SNAPpers Bryce Wedig, Andrew Mattson, Yanbo Pan, and Rishika Porandla discussed the technological and scientific advances the launch of the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope will enable. Read it [here](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap/why-build-a-4b-space-telescope-892bf55eed78). Last week, Brandon Emanuel León wrote about the importance of publicly accessible science in his article "**Science on Tap: Serving up Science to Community Members in Miami.**" The piece, which you can read [here](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap/science-on-tap-serving-up-science-to-community-members-in-miami-5d289397f919), highlights an exciting event hosted at a brewery in Miami, where community members came together to learn about and share the scientific research happening at the University of Miami.

Be sure to [follow us on Medium](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap) to read the upcoming pieces!

#### Stance on Science

Stance on Science participants are asking science policy-related questions of candidates running in local, state, and national races during the 2026 elections. State teams have started to develop their questionnaires, plan candidate outreach, and set up town halls in their communities. **This is a great time to get involved and make sure science policy issues important to your community are included as teams begin to contact campaigns, especially with November election efforts ramping up in many states!**

Check out responses we've already received from candidates across several states at the [Stance on Science website](https://snapcoalition.org/initiatives/stance-on-science), and read more about the initiative in recent press mentions, including one from this month in the [*Concord Monitor*](https://www.concordmonitor.com/2026/08/20/researchers-engage-nh-politics/).

**We still need volunteers!** If you're interested in helping out as a state lead or a volunteer, please fill out this [sign-up form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSem6bVuZnK7eWls-CTMpxWxaxHpuElNj4oOXIvLkgDYRO6YNg/viewform). Please spread the word! We are particularly looking for constituents in Colorado, Hawaii, Idaho, Maryland, North Dakota, South Dakota, and Virginia to help these teams grow!
