#### 2026 SNAP Congressional District Visits

The 2026 SNAP Congressional District Visits were [mentioned in Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johndrake/2026/10/29/who-should-decide-what-science-gets-funded-young-scientists-are-organizing/)! Due to the time-sensitive nature of this initiative, we realize that not every interested individual was able to participate in meetings with their representatives. However, there is still an opportunity to write and publish Op-Eds on topics such as the benefits of fundamental research, the potential implications of rules like OMB-2026-0034, and the importance of prioritizing both accountability and stability of funding! **If you are interested and have not yet filled out [this op-ed interest form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfeRmD1YYFLeErNy80NA7SQ-Jc3cPGUOO1LSG5NINI560WXLA/viewform?usp=header), please do!** Upon filling out this form, we will provide additional resources to help you write, including 1) advice from the former New York Times Opinion Editor Clay Risen, 2) SNAP guides to writing an Op-Ed and 3) assistance with editing and/or help pitching to outlets.

#### Science Policy in a SNAP - SNAP Blog

You can access our blog, Science Policy in a SNAP, [on our website](https://snapcoalition.org/blog/) or give us a follow [on Medium](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap), where you'll hear from a variety of SNAP member voices on diverse science and science-policy topics.

In late October, Erin Cullinane, Andrew Ramirez, Sari Terrazas published "[The Hurdles of Treating Cancer Beyond Science and Innovation](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap/the-hurdles-of-treating-cancer-beyond-science-and-innovation-2db1fb2adcd9)," where they discuss the importance of considering the impact of new insurance rules, costs, and recent policy changes on the ability for cancer patients to access new treatments and join clinical trials. This month, Doga Tasdemir and Andrew Ramirez published "[Funding, Trust, and the Next Pandemic](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap/funding-trust-and-the-next-pandemic-05bece35fb1b)," discussing the AIDS epidemic and the COVID-19 pandemic, arguing that steady research funding and public trust in science are vital aspects of epidemic and pandemic preparedness. Recently, Sneha Rao and Celine Tsoi published, "[Wombs, Wealth, and Worth: Exploring the ethical implications of reproductive technologies](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap/wombs-wealth-and-worth-exploring-the-ethical-implications-of-reproductive-technologies-edc6ea05e03f)," which provides an overview of modern assisted reproductive technologies, discusses some of the ethical considerations that arise with these technologies, and explores some approaches to better prepare for the future of reproductive science.

Be sure to [follow us on Medium](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap) to read the upcoming pieces!

#### Stance on Science

[Stance on Science](https://snapcoalition.org/initiatives/stance-on-science) participants asked science policy-related questions of candidates running in local, state, and national races during the 2026 elections. We received a total of **5,542 responses from 1,028 candidates to 366 questions across all 50 states and DC, and our initiative was publicized by 16 local and regional news outlets.** Candidate's responses are still up on our website; there's still time to see how your elected officials answered our questions! Stay tuned for additional information from Stance, including an analysis of the initiative.

#### Check Out Our SciPol Course

Our SciPol 101 Course is up, and we are excited to see how the modules are being used so far! This open-access curriculum to educate and empower scientists to engage in science policy and advocacy is [available on our website](https://snapcoalition.org/initiatives/scipol-courses) for use and covers five topics: What is Science Policy?, How Government Works, Writing for Science Policy, Science Communication, and Science Diplomacy. Continue to check them out, use and modify the modules as you need, and let us know what you think!

#### SNAPStart: Mini-Grant Program for New SciPol Orgs

**[Eight incredible science policy organizations](https://snapcoalition.org/initiatives/snapstart)** were recently selected as the inaugural recipients for our SNAPStart mini-grant program. It was an incredibly difficult review process; we received 24 total applications from new and revived groups in 19 different states. The next round of applications is slated to open in December; watch our social media and our website for updates!
